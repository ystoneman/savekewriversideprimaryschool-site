Kew evidence · Checked 5 October 2026

What the current records establish.

Find an original source ↓

Falling pupil numbers and recurring costs give the council a serious case for change. The public evidence does not yet settle whether closure is better than a funded alternative. Closure remains proposed; respond by 16 October without waiting for missing records.

Explore further: Demand · Closure costs · Lessons from other schools

Published forecast · unresolved records

The annual forecast gap is £212,417.

That is for 2026/27. The accumulated deficit is forecast to reach £457,702 by 2028/29. This indicative forecast is not current cash or a closure saving. The approved budget is still needed to resolve conflicting reserve wording.

Understand the budget and missing records · Original FAQ Q5–6, page 4

Current guidance · conditional alternatives

The free-school rationale needs updating.

September 2026 rules provide a route for a replacement maintained school; altering a surviving school is another route. Neither establishes a willing partner, funding or suitable premises at Kew. A current, costed assessment is still needed.

What each alternative must demonstrate · Current DfE guidance · Council FAQ Q4

Council commitment · delivery details unresolved

A Darell guarantee needs delivery details.

If closure is approved, current Kew pupils whose parents want one are guaranteed a Darell place. Staffing, support and transition arrangements still need establishing. Missing public records do not show that Darell cannot meet children’s needs.

Practical family questions · Council commitments · Equality analysis, pages 2 and 15

Demand: dated counts and changing forecasts

The January 2026 census counts 84 pupils at Kew, excluding nursery; the leaflet uses 87 and the Kew FAQ also uses 85. These need a dated year-group admissions/leavers bridge, rather than one being silently selected as today’s count.

For the Kew planning area in 2027/28, the 2025 SCAP forecast is 598; the leaflet projects 638. The difference is 40 pupils between forecast versions, not an observed forecast error. One matched historical check was close overall (651 forecast, 654 counted) but low for Reception (88 versus 97). That does not establish long-term accuracy.

Request the current cohort matrix, forecast versions and housing-use/delivery assumptions. Proposed homes do not automatically mean pupils at Kew. Check the matched census and forecast sources · DfE planning-area forecast data · Leaflet page 5.

Closure costs, PFI and the site: what is still unknown

The £69,640 school PFI charge in 2025/26 is not the council’s whole liability or an automatically removable cost. The audited £42.937 million remaining-payment figure covers six primary schools at March 2025, before future adjustments.

A net comparison needs Kew’s governing contracts and site rights, avoidable versus continuing costs, receiving-school funding, workforce, SEND, travel and transition costs. Continuing PFI costs can occur under both options; they do not prove zero savings. Freehold labels do not establish unrestricted disposal rights. The reviewed evidence does not establish a PFI or land motive for closure.

School charge and financial scope · Audited accounts, Note 41 · FAQ Q9–10 · Exact remaining cost questions.

Have a useful source or correction? Share one dated public record or suggest one follow-up question for Yann’s review, not a council response. Leave out private messages, family details and identifying child information. Use confidential school or council channels for an individual child’s needs.

Which missing records would settle the questions? Missing public evidence does not prove illegality, predetermination or unsuitable provision.

What we can learn · Selected London cases

Four London schools kept teaching.

Here’s what changed the decisions.

St John the Divine · June 2025

The case for moving pupils did not stand up.

The adjudicator found process deficiencies. They also found too little evidence that enough families would transfer to make the receiving school sustainable. The closure proposal was rejected.

Read the case · Adjudicator’s decision (PDF, §§44–61, 68)

Fenstanton & Holy Trinity · July 2025

The decisions had not been made lawfully.

Unlawful conditions and decision procedures led the adjudicators to reject the linked proposals. Both schools remained open: two schools in one decision.

Read the case · Adjudicators’ decision (PDF, §§29–39)

Pooles Park, Islington · 2023 decision

A willing sponsor offered a viable alternative.

A minister kept the academy order in place. The minister accepted the proposed academy model, extra SEND provision and community benefits. Teaching continued through the academy, which opened in November 2024.

Read the case · Judgment (§§1, 52–53) · DfE register

Our takeaway for Kew

Make the alternative specific.

Ask for a costed option that keeps teaching on site. It needs realistic pupil assumptions and a credible plan to deliver it. Use focused consultation questions to find missing evidence and specific flaws in the proposal.

The school types and legal routes differ. These decisions do not give every parent a right to appeal. Nor do they prove that an alternative at Kew is viable.

The answers that matter next

Unanswered questions

Eight priorities for this parent-led site, about keeping Kew open and children’s next steps.

You can respond now without waiting for answers. Find the official response route.

01

What would it take to keep Kew open?

Still to establish

Known: The March 2026 revenue reserve and five years of reported balances are available. FAQ Q5 now publishes indicative annual gaps and balances; the approved underlying budget and ledger remain sought. The leaflet acknowledges class and staffing changes. But the evidence we reviewed does not show what pupil number would secure the school’s future.

The school publishes capacity for 210 alongside mixed-age teaching. The council’s January 2026 model lists 210 permanent and 140 temporary admission-based places, both dated September 2025. A 105-pupil model—15 in each of seven cohorts—is an illustrative scenario, not a verified current capacity or financial target.

Still unanswered: How do these capacity measures relate to today’s classes, staffing and pupils by year group? What yearly income and costs explain why the school’s budget is forecast to get worse? How many extra pupils, in which years, by which funding census dates, and with what interim support would make the smaller school sustainable? Would a credible balanced-budget plan change the recommendation, or would wider place-planning reasons still decide it?

Read the financial explanation · What a recovery plan needs

Requested 23 September 2026: the school’s approved budget, monitoring and aggregate pupil records, and the council’s annual forecast and assumptions through 2028/29.

02

What would each outcome mean for our children?

Still to establish

Known: The leaflet offers current Kew pupils a Darell place if closure goes ahead and their parents want one. The council FAQ promises to keep siblings together. The borough equality assessment says school-specific work should follow.

Still unanswered: How would teaching, staffed classes, friendships and trusted support compare with a realistic plan to stay at Kew? What would support look like for children with special educational needs or disabilities, including those without an Education, Health and Care Plan? How would sibling arrangements, offers, deadlines, safe journeys, wraparound care and funded transition support work? How will staff and support be maintained during the uncertainty, and could any further reorganisation mean another move?

The council promises curriculum continuity, emotional support and tailored SEND transition. Ask for the year groups that would transfer, staffed classes, specialist support, travel and childcare arrangements, with dates and committed funding. The current Year 6 leaves primary education in summer 2027.

The public material we reviewed does not include a Kew-specific assessment. That does not prove none exists or that Darell cannot provide suitable support. Keep each child’s details in private school or council channels. Find practical family guidance.

Requested 23 September 2026: the Kew-specific equality assessment and an existing receiving-provision plan covering aggregate classes, staffing, specialist places, transport and costs.

03

Why has Kew been selected?

Still to establish

Known: The leaflet explains the council’s reasons for proposing closure. Those reasons need to be tested against the comparable evidence and alternatives.

Still unanswered: What criteria, assessment and any scores or weights led to selecting Kew, and were they applied consistently? How were educational quality, inclusion, buildings, demand and financial recovery considered? What pupil transfers does the receiving-school budget assume, and does the plan remain viable if fewer families choose it?

Compare the same dates and measures: annual results, reserves and future forecasts are different things. A larger deficit elsewhere would not by itself establish inconsistent treatment. See the other Richmond proposal · Check the educational evidence.

04

What would closure save, and what happens to the site?

Still to establish

Known: The school’s 2025/26 PFI charge is reported. PFI is the long-term building finance and maintenance arrangement. A school charge is not the net saving from closure.

Still unanswered: Which costs would stop, continue or arise under closure versus keeping teaching at Kew? Include building payments, any contract variation or termination costs, redundancy, transport and receiving-school adaptations. What future site use is assumed, what happens at the contract’s end, and what would closure mean for any nursery or other existing users?

An exit fee is a question to investigate, not an established cost. Continuing PFI payments would not by themselves prove that closure saves nothing. Read the cost comparison.

Requested 23 September 2026: the council’s existing closure-versus-continuation cost comparison, Kew PFI governing-body agreement, and relevant closure, site-use and payment provisions.

05

Which workable alternatives have actually been costed?

Still to establish

Known: The leaflet gives reasons for rejecting alternatives. Its categorical free-school rationale is superseded by September 2026 guidance. A current appraisal must distinguish the existing-school route and the current replacement-school guidance.

Still unanswered: What would a smaller school, shared leadership, federation or amalgamation cost? Could suitable early-years or community use of spare rooms contribute net income? What permissions, willing partners, staffing and start-up funding would each option need? Has a funded recovery period with clear milestones been assessed, and why was each option rejected?

A legal route or a spare room does not prove there is a workable, funded alternative. Explore the possible routes.

Requested 23 September 2026: the council’s existing options appraisal and Kew decision records, with an explanation of routes that could retain teaching at Kew.

06

What support and time would recruitment receive?

Still to establish

Known: The council FAQ says applications and admissions can continue during this pre-statutory consultation. Enquiries, visits, applications and actual enrolments are different stages; neither a place nor the school’s future is guaranteed.

Still unanswered: What admissions, promotion, collaboration and financial support has the council offered, when, and with what results? What resources and time would a school-coordinated recruitment effort receive now? Which current applications are likely to become starts, in which year groups, and how do they compare with departures? How will the proposal’s effect on confidence and applications be assessed?

Any plan must work within admissions rules and respect families’ choices. See what a recruitment plan needs · Ask the school about visits and places.

07

Do the forecasts reflect current admissions and housing demand?

Still to establish

Known: One matched planning-area forecast check is available; the June report and later leaflet use different 2031/32 area estimates. Planned homes do not automatically become pupils at Kew.

Still unanswered: Which versions, current pupil counts and assumptions underpin the forecasts? How accurate have school and area forecasts been over matching periods? For developments such as Stag Brewery and the former Homebase site, what delivery status, occupation dates, pupil yields and school-level effects are assumed? How would different outcomes affect Kew and the receiving schools’ places by year group?

Housing demand matters beyond the PFI contract’s end date too. Read the forecast check and its limits.

Requested 23 September 2026: current and June Kew-area forecast versions, assumptions and accuracy checks, plus dated aggregate pupil counts from the school.

08

When will parents receive written answers and evidence?

Still to establish

Known: Requests for records were sent on 23 September. Sending a request does not establish that records have been disclosed or that the council agrees with the questions.

Still unanswered: Who will publish written answers, supporting records and the meeting record, and by when? How will unresolved questions and parents’ evidence be represented to decision-makers? If material new evidence arrives late, what time and opportunity will families have to consider it and respond?

A request does not automatically extend the consultation. You can respond now without waiting for answers. Use the official response route and check the current timetable.
