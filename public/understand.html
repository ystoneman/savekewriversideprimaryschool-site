Three findings to start with

What the numbers tell us.

Kew’s pupil numbers fell faster than the borough’s.

36.6% down at Kew, compared with 5.5% across Richmond’s 45 primary-phase schools, from May 2022 to May 2025, excluding nursery. The figures show the difference, not its cause: a recovery plan needs to explain what could change locally. Check the pupil figures.

There was a reserve. A later deficit is projected.

Kew had a £231,685.09 revenue reserve on 31 March 2026. The council projects more than £400,000 accumulated deficit by 2028/29. The annual budget connecting them is missing from the reviewed records. Neither headline establishes long-term viability or the amount needed to keep the school open. Check the financial evidence.

The forecast was close overall, but too low for Reception.

For 2025/26, the Kew-area forecast was 651 pupils; January 2026 counted 654. Reception was 88 forecast, 97 counted. This is one period across three schools, excluding nursery—not a forecast for Kew Riverside Primary School alone or a long-term accuracy test. Later forecasts still need checking. Check the forecast evidence.

Why did the later forecast change?

For the same Kew planning area and target year 2031/32, the June 2026 school organisation report projected 514 pupils. The Kew consultation leaflet projected 597, an increase of 83. This forecast revision is not itself a measured forecasting error. Which population, housing, admissions or method assumptions changed? And which version is the proposal and school budget based on? June report (original ↗︎) · consultation leaflet (original ↗︎).

How this forecast check was calculated

The DfE forecast file was filtered to Primary, planning-area code 3180007 and time period 202526. The January census counts were summed for school URNs 133343, 102884, 102921, Reception–Year 6, excluding nursery. Actual minus forecast is +3 pupils overall and +9 in Reception. With the separate signed error formula (forecast − actual) ÷ actual × 100, these are about -0.46% and -9.28%; a percentage error is not the same measure as the count difference. Longer-horizon, repeated checks remain to be requested.

A useful question for the council: provide its checks by forecast vintage, horizon and year group, and explain the assumptions behind the revised Kew-area series. Read the consultation questions.

What would closure actually save?

The DfE records a £69,640 PFI charge in Kew Riverside Primary School's 2025/26 expenditure. That school charge is not the council's entire contract liability or a saving that would automatically follow closure. Which costs would cease, continue or transfer—and what new transition, receiving-school, support or transport costs would arise? The net comparison and Kew-specific contract terms were not in the reviewed public records. DfE expenditure history (original ↗︎) · See the cost audit question.

A question to use in your response

The missing financial test: ask for the approved 2026/27 budget, monitoring and annual forecast that reconcile the dated reserve with the council's projected deficit. Also ask for a comparable net cost for each realistic alternative. Use this in a consultation response.

Sources, missing values and a conflicting school graphic

Use the academic year to compare results, not the date a page was published. This collection uses final DfE school/area results and eligible-pupil metadata, with the inspection’s historical subject table and the newer Darell announcement clearly distinguished. It does not infer 2024 cohort counts from a three-year total.

Kew’s school-published 2025 graphic gives maths as 76% expected and 29% higher, while final DfE gives 73% and 33%. We use the final DfE values; the reason for the difference has not been established. The inspection describes Kew’s 2024 combined result as close to average, although its percentage is numerically above England’s. There are no 2024/25 KS1-to-KS2 progress measures because that cohort’s earlier assessments were cancelled.

Downloads keep missing values as missing; they do not turn them into zeros. The selected years do not bridge the years when assessments were cancelled. School comparisons cannot separate the effect of teaching from the make-up of each cohort or other influences.

How mixed-age learning is planned at Kew

The school’s curriculum explanation describes a two-year Cycle A/Cycle B plan. It says the plan is meant to cover the National Curriculum while building on earlier learning. Its current overview shows Cycle B for 2025/26 and Cycle A for 2026/27.

A concrete example: the Year 1/2 curriculum map, PDF page 3 lists the Great Fire of London for Cycle A’s first autumn half-term. For Cycle B, it lists the Gunpowder Plot/Remembrance Day. Alternating topics illustrates the school’s coverage plan. It does not by itself show that every child avoids missed or repeated learning.

How these comparisons were made
  • Dates stay separate. May school-roll histories, May 2025 capacity and January 2026 year groups answer different questions. None is a live September headcount.
  • Comparable scope. The borough tables cover 45 mainstream state-funded primary-phase schools, including infant and junior schools, with nursery excluded. They are not a ranking of school quality. Capacity excludes places in SEN units and resourced provision; those are reported separately.
  • Local selection. Kew Riverside Primary School, Darell and The King’s are the three schools in Kew planning area 3180007 in the capacity dataset. The King’s name is used in the latest records; older documents call it The Queen’s.
  • Reorganisations and sites. School histories follow the stable school establishment identifier where three schools changed their unique reference number. Thomson House’s two site records are added into one school total. All 45 schools have four years of data.
  • Calculated figures. Occupancy = pupils ÷ reported capacity × 100. Trend change = (last roll ÷ first roll − 1) × 100. Percentages are rounded to one decimal; totals are calculated from unrounded counts.
  • Limits. These charts cannot show causes, whether places are available now or how many pupils are needed for financial viability. Funding and housing scenarios need more verified evidence.
