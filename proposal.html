Richmond Council proposes closing Kew Riverside Primary School at the end of the 2026/27 school year, from 1 September 2027. No final decision has been made.

Why we use 16 October: the council-linked response form gives that exact deadline. The public leaflet’s initial-consultation row says September–November, while the general FAQ says October. If closure is approved

The council promises a Darell place for current Kew Riverside Primary School pupils whose parents want one. Parents can also apply to other schools with vacancies. Detailed transition plans for pupils still matter. Read the council’s offer.

If closure does not proceed

The proposed closure date would not be an instruction to close. The council would need to confirm the next steps. An alternative would need agreed delivery, funding and pupil assumptions; this collection does not establish that any alternative is approved or viable.

02 / Evidence and open questions

Choose one question worth asking.

These questions draw on the council’s leaflet and the government’s guidance on closing schools. They are questions I suggest asking, not official requirements. Pick what matters most to you. You can respond without waiting for answers.

If Kew closed, where would my child go?

If closure is approved, the council offers every current Kew pupil a Darell place where parents want one (leaflet, PDF page 2). Its general FAQ, PDF page 8 also promises to keep siblings together. Ask how classes, staffing, support and safe journeys would work for Kew families.

What we’ve asked for about children’s needs →

Would my child learn as well if Kew closed?

The council argues that closure would prevent education suffering as budgets shrink (leaflet, PDF page 6). Ask it to compare teaching, support and continuity under closure with a realistic plan to keep Kew open. Today’s results cannot tell us how either option would affect your child.

Kew’s test results and inspection →

How could a £232,000 reserve become a £400,000-plus deficit?

Kew had a £231,685 revenue reserve on 31 March 2026. The Kew FAQ Q5, page 4 now projects a £212,417 annual gap in 2026/27, £19,268 left at March 2027 and an accumulated £457,702 deficit by 2028/29. Those forecasts conflict with the leaflet’s earlier exhaustion wording. The received final budget summary now provides the income and spending totals. Ask for the detailed ledger, current monitoring, commitments and assumptions behind them.

The budget explained →

Would closing Kew actually save money?

The projected £400,000-plus deficit is a total built up by 2028/29, not a yearly loss or a closure saving. The leaflet gives no net closure saving (leaflet, PDF page 3). Ask which costs would stop, continue or arise, and what closure would save overall.

What closing would cost →

How sure is the council that numbers will keep falling?

The leaflet reports 145 pupils in 2021/22 and 87 in 2025/26. It forecasts 73 in 2028/29, then a small rise (leaflet, PDF page 5). Ask how past forecasts compared with actual numbers and which new housing they include. A key test is whether the need for places is forecast to rise over the medium to long term (DfE guidance, PDF page 8).

One past forecast check for the Kew area →

Could Kew stay open another way?

The leaflet briefly explains why five alternatives were rejected (leaflet, PDF page 6), without a costed comparison. Ask for that comparison, including a smaller intake or using part of the site differently (DfE guidance, PDF page 8). No alternative has yet been shown here to be funded and workable.

03 / Public responsibilities

Who will decide Kew Riverside Primary School’s future?

Start with the institution and the decision stage. Being named here does not mean someone supports closure. A person’s office, their public position and their recorded vote are different things.

Richmond’s Education and Children’s Services Committee

The consultation leaflet gives both the decision to proceed and the final decision to this committee as a whole. Officers prepare the evidence; the chair leads its meetings.

Decision stages · Committee remit (PDF) · Current membership

Committee members, officers and representatives

Committee members

The published remit provides for 11 councillors, two voting diocesan members and a non-voting parent governor representative. For each meeting, check who attended, any substitutes and who was entitled to vote. Being a member does not show how anyone voted.

Council and AfC officers

Achieving for Children provides children’s services for the council and draws up school organisation proposals for it. Officers prepare evidence and run the process. The FAQ explains AfC’s role.

Representatives and governors

Ward councillors and the MP can represent residents. Those roles do not by themselves give a committee vote. The school’s governing body has a collective role. Relevant official statements can be added when verified.

04 / The decision record

Record what was actually decided.

The records we reviewed show authority to start consultation, not a decision to close Kew. This page does not link any person to a closure vote.

Read the dated decisions and official records

On 11 June 2026, the committee recorded a general delegation to the Executive Director of Children’s Services. It lets the Director, in consultation with the committee’s Chair, take assessed school-organisation proposals to pre-statutory consultation. That is a route to starting consultation, not a decision to close Kew. The record reviewed here does not show when that delegation was used for Kew. No Kew closure determination or individual closure vote has been verified. The 10 September meeting record contains no Kew closure resolution. The November agenda is not yet published.

05 / Use the official process

Make your contribution count.

Start with your consultation response.

Respond directly through the official form by the stated date, 16 October 2026. As checked on 23 September, it asks your connection to the school, your view and reasons, and offers space for alternatives and comments. It does not ask for a name or postcode.

The leaflet also gives the institutional email schoolorganisation@achievingforchildren.org.uk and a postal route. Read the official response instructions.

For a relevant committee item, follow the council’s rules for speaking and written questions. Check the agenda, who can take part and the deadlines to register before you apply.

Keep your points to the proposal, the evidence and the effects on pupils. Respect everyone’s privacy. If you write to someone, use official channels.
