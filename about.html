Our family · Yann & Sofiya

We moved here for this school.

We’re Yann and Sofiya. Our daughter has only just started Reception at Kew Riverside Primary School, and we’re already facing the possibility of the school closing. We’re shocked, and we imagine many of you are too.

Our family’s experience, in our own words · Updated 22 September 2026.

Get in touch privatelyShare your own experience →

The community we were looking for

We spent the last academic year visiting schools — at least a dozen, both state and private, from this area towards central London. For us, none came close to the feeling of community we found at Kew Riverside Primary School.

It was the warmth of the teachers and staff. The diversity, inclusion and celebration of different backgrounds and cultures. The way people seemed truly present, mentally and emotionally, and so intentional about how they taught and cared for each child.

We loved the smaller classes and mixed-age classrooms. We saw a place where children could get help catching up in one area and be encouraged to stretch themselves in another. What mattered most to us was how close, supportive and connected it felt. The school community felt like an extension of the family we have at home.

A little like cousins

Something from our first parent–teacher meeting really stayed with Sofiya. Our Reception teacher and SENDCo lead talked about how families don’t always have aunts, uncles, cousins and different generations around them day to day anymore.

She highlighted how recreating some of that at Kew Riverside Primary School helps build a community that feels like an extended family, where children can learn from people of different ages, backgrounds and experiences. A little “like cousins”. That meant a lot to us.

This is our family’s experience. For the documented provision and its limits, read how Kew plans mixed-age learning and the balanced research summary.

Curiosity, care and a wish to contribute

We want our child to find happiness and fulfilment in being curious about how the world around her works — and the world within her, too. In being eager to learn, and wanting to contribute to the people around her and her community.

Doing well matters to us. But having to be “the best” isn’t what we want to drive her. From what we’ve seen, the school embraces that spirit through its teaching and its caring, supportive environment. It’s a big part of why we chose it.

These are values we hope children carry with them: into their homes, their friendships, and all the communities they may become part of as adults.

For us, Kew Riverside Primary School brings all of this together while maintaining an exceptionally good quality of education. We see warmth, inclusion and high expectations going hand in hand, with children supported to achieve their best. That combination means so much to us.

A place to put down roots

Sofiya found and chose this school for our family. We moved here specifically for it, hoping to stay for all seven years, from Reception through Year 6. We hoped this would be a place to put down roots, build relationships and give our daughter continuity. That is why keeping this community matters so much to us.

I’m Yann, and I started this website to help keep the school open and work with other parents, carers and neighbours. Below is what I’m asking for and how I take responsibility for the site.

What I want to happen

My first preference is to keep Kew Riverside Primary School open and preserve the qualities that brought us here. I want the council to publish and assess costed alternatives alongside the effects on pupils and families.

Keeping the current school open is one outcome. Keeping teaching on the site under a different arrangement is another. Protecting pupils’ continuity if closure goes ahead is a third, separate outcome. I want alternatives tested against smaller classes, mixed-age learning, emotional support and relationships, as well as finances and pupil numbers.

I have not established that an alternative is funded or viable. The site includes the council’s published explanations and the questions that remain.

Who is responsible for this site?

I created and run this website in a personal capacity. I’m responsible for its factual and editorial decisions, including corrections and which contributions are published. I use AI tools to help with research and writing. I am still responsible for what this site says and for correcting mistakes.

This is my initiative. It is not operated by the school, Richmond Council, the PTA or a formally agreed parent group. I do not claim to speak for all parents, governors or the wider community.

I’d like to work with other affected families and hear from anyone already coordinating a response. If a group becomes involved, I will describe its actual role and name people only with their agreement. No organiser list, partner endorsement or collective mandate is implied by this website.

A clear position. Evidence you can check.

I want the school to stay open. That position is separate from whether a particular claim is well supported. The source library links to original records. It separates reviewed documents from routes we have not read, and keeps dates attached to forecasts and observations.

The current proposal and timetable were checked on 21 September 2026. I’m responsible for keeping that status note up to date. It is a dated research snapshot, so check the council’s current instructions before acting.

Factual corrections and public-role concerns go through private human review. Contributions expressing a different view on closure are welcome in community letters, under the same publication rules.

There are different ways to contribute.

Write a community letter to share your experience. You can also share an idea, public evidence or a meeting question, or add your name to the supporter statement.

Named support requires separate consent and a private confirmation of your public name and the exact statement before a person approves it. A letter does not automatically add you to the supporter list. Letters need your publication permission and human review; any substantive wording changes require your agreement.

These contributions are separate from the council’s consultation. Respond directly to the council by its stated date, 16 October 2026.
