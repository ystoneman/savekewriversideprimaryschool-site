Supporting notes and sources for Yann Stoneman’s 21 October letter

This note supports Yann’s letter asking for a supported recovery programme; it is not a separate community contribution.

Supporting funding note

The DfE’s July 2026 allocation figures, after academy recoupment and other deductions, show Richmond’s Dedicated Schools Grant allocation £6.28 million higher for 2026/27 than for 2025/26, including £1.94 million more in the schools block. DfE allocation statements for Richmond.

These are nominal comparisons. Separate grants have been incorporated into 2026/27 funding, so the figures do not establish a like-for-like increase or money freely available to Kew. DfE guidance on incorporated grants.

Separately, the council’s budget book describes a projected reduction of approximately 58% in core government funding by 2028/29. These figures concern different funding streams and periods. The question is how those pressures affect Kew specifically, and what support for recovery has been considered. Council budget book, page 7 (PDF).

Capacity and recovery details

At 30 pupils per class, the current four-class teaching model gives a ceiling of 120. The 84 pupils cited in the meeting represent 70% of that figure, compared with 40% against 210 published places. The council’s 140 temporary admission-based places are a third, distinct measure. These comparisons do not establish available admissions places or financial viability. The request is for the council to explain its measures and the pupil numbers needed to sustain the actual staffing structure.

The recovery assessment requested in the letter should consider proposed savings, recurring income, appropriate use of spare space and realistic pupil assumptions together, with practical support for implementation and a Kew-specific assessment of equality, SEND and wider pupil impacts.

Sources for the letter

  • DfE historical balances: annual surpluses in 2022/23–2024/25 (JSON).
  • School financing scheme, section 4.3 (PDF) and Appendix 6: recovery support (PDF).
  • Council consultation leaflet: class and staffing changes (PDF).
  • Council falling-rolls table: permanent and temporary admission-based measures (PDF). The letter’s 120 figure is a four-class teaching-model ceiling, not this table’s admissions measure.
  • BBC report containing the council leader’s core-funding explanation.
  • Kew FAQ, questions 5 and 6 (PDF).

The meeting account and personal reaction are Yann’s account of the 21 October 2026 meeting. The recovery programme and additional year are his requests, not agreed council commitments.

Letter guidelines and publication review

A letter is your opinion.

  1. Choose who can see it. Public display and council sharing are independent choices: you can choose either, both or neither. If you publish, you can also let Yann quote short extracts beyond this website.
  2. Screening before publication. If you allow publication, a routine, relevant letter may appear after automated screening. Letters with spam, impersonation, threats, abuse, private contact details, sensitive disclosures or personal allegations are held for human review. So are letters with unclear authorship or permissions. Nothing is sent to the council automatically.
  3. Published letters appear in the feed above. Publishing a letter does not mean it is endorsed or fact-checked. It is not a verified signature or a council response.

We apply the same rules to supportive and critical views. If a parent mentions their child's first name in an ordinary way, that alone does not stop publication. We may change only spacing and formatting without asking you. Letters are checked periodically. We cannot promise when a letter will be reviewed, or that it will be published.

For adults writing in their own words. For an urgent concern, or one about a particular child, please use official school and safeguarding channels.
